Why Velora

Built for Apple silicon, not ported to it.

Plex and Jellyfin are excellent media servers that run almost anywhere. Velora makes a different trade: it runs only on the Mac and only for Apple devices, so it can use the hardware and frameworks Apple builds into every one of them.

Download for Mac

Native from server to screen

The server is a Swift app built for macOS, and every client is SwiftUI with Apple's own player, Liquid Glass, Live Activities, Top Shelf, SharePlay and system sharing. Nothing is a web view in a wrapper.

Apple silicon does the heavy lifting

Transcoding uses VideoToolbox, the hardware media engine in every Apple silicon Mac, with no extra license to unlock it. Audiobook voices run on-device with MLX.

Quiet, efficient, already yours

A Mac mini on a shelf is silent and sips power when idle. You don't need a separate NAS or a PC running all day.

No account with anyone

Velora doesn't sign you in to a company cloud. Your devices talk to your Mac directly, and nothing leaves your network unless you turn on a feature that needs to.

More than video

Books and PDFs with Apple Pencil notes, audiobooks with chapters and speed, synced lyrics, and Create Audio for turning documents into listenable audio.

Ready for AI assistants

A built-in MCP server lets an assistant you choose search your library and control playback, with scoped tokens and no destructive tools.

Which should you choose?

  • Choose Velora if your home runs on Apple devices, you have an Apple silicon Mac, and you want everything to feel like it was made by the same hands, without a cloud account.
  • Choose Plex if you need apps on almost every device ever made, or want its built-in free streaming channels.
  • Choose Jellyfin if you want a free, open-source server on Linux or a NAS, or a big plugin ecosystem.
